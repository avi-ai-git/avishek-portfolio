# Case Study Structure — AV.AI Portfolio

One reusable structure applied consistently across all case studies.

---

## Structure

### 1. One-line thesis
A single sentence that tells the reader exactly what this is and why it matters.
Format: [What I built/did] + [what it proves or who it's for].

### 2. Context
2–3 sentences of background. Who commissioned it? What was the setting? What made this interesting or unusual?

### 3. Problem / Challenge
The core challenge framed as a question or tension. Keep it human and specific — not a generic "we needed to improve X."

### 4. My Role
List of specific responsibilities. Not a job title. What did Avishek actually do?

### 5. Process
The workflow or approach. Ordered steps where possible. What decisions were made and why?

### 6. Tools / Workflow
Named tools, organized by function. Be specific — not "AI tools" but Midjourney for image generation, ElevenLabs for voice.

### 7. Output
What was produced? Be concrete. List deliverables.

### 8. Result / Proof
What happened? Numbers where real and available. Links where live. Festival selections. Verifiable outcomes.
Do not invent metrics. Mark missing proof clearly as TODO.

### 9. What this demonstrates for employers
Translate the project into hiring language. What skills, roles, and contexts does this prove?

### 10. Assets
List available media: videos, screenshots, live URLs, LinkedIn posts.

### 11. TODOs / Missing proof
What is still needed to make this case study complete?

---

## Rules

- Do not invent numbers, clients, or outcomes.
- If something is missing, write TODO.
- Preserve nuance — do not compress detailed process into vague summaries.
- Write in first person where the case study text is Avishek speaking.
- Keep each section focused: context is not the problem; role is not the process.
